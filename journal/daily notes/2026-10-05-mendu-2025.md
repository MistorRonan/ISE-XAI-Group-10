## TL;DR

The paper argues that current pretraining data filters (keyword blocklists, Perspective API, HateBERT) are too blunt. They work on short sentences, use a binary safe/toxic label and often remove useful text, such as a suicide-prevention article, just because it contains words like "self-harm". The authors (Microsoft) propose a three-way taxonomy (**Safe / Topical / Toxic**) across five harm categories and build two filters on it: **TTP**, a GPT-4o prompt, and **HarmFormer**, a fine-tuned Longformer. Running TTP on 3M pages shows that 2.1–4.1% of Common Crawl, C4 and FineWeb is still toxic even after their existing filtering. For us, it's a recent, strong, black-box filter whose removal decisions are never explained, which is exactly the gap our project targets.

## Key takeaways

- **Taxonomy:** five harm categories (Hate & Violence, Ideological Harm, Sexual, Illegal Activities, Self-Inflicted Harm), each split into **Topical** (neutral discussion, e.g. news on riots, academic analysis of conspiracy theories, which should be kept) and **Toxic** (harmful intent, which should be removed).
- **TTP (prompt-based filter):** built on GPT-4o with few-shot and chain-of-thought prompting. It was evaluated on TTP-Eval: 491 web pages labelled by two in-house experts (Krippendorff's α = 0.77). Toxic F1 is 0.83, against 0.63 for Perspective API. The prompt doesn't transfer to other LLMs (Gemma 2 27B: F1 0.35).
- **HarmFormer (model-based filter):** a Longformer (1024 tokens) with five multi-task heads, trained on 253K pages **labelled by TTP, not by humans**. Toxic F1 is 0.85, but it is much weaker on the categories closest to our topic: Hate & Violence F1 0.51 and Ideological Harm F1 0.61.
- **Main error source:** most false positives are "reporting" pages, i.e. pages that report hate or violence without endorsing it. The filters struggle to tell *discussing* apart from *promoting*.
- **Prevalence in pretraining data (Table 8):** toxic content makes up 4.1% of Common Crawl, 2.1% of C4 and 3.9% of FineWeb. Topical content is higher in C4 (6.8%) and FineWeb (10.6%) than in Common Crawl (3.4%). The authors argue that keyword blocklists both miss toxic content and strip out valuable context.
- **HAVOC benchmark:** 10,376 open-ended prefixes used to test six base LLMs (Gemma 2, Llama 3.2, Mistral). 26.7% of generations "leak" toxic content overall, rising to 76% when the prefix itself is toxic. Sexual content leaks the most.
- **Main argument:** filtering should be intent-aware (topical vs. toxic) rather than binary, so that harmful content is removed while socially important discussion is kept.

## Why it matters for our project

1. **Motivation evidence.** It directly supports our topic description's claim that existing filters are crude. Keyword blocklists conflate harmful intent with educational content, and Perspective API generalises poorly. It's a recent (2025), top-venue source for the Introduction and Background.
2. **It filters without explaining.** HarmFormer is a black-box transformer, and the authors' only error analysis is manual inspection of false positives. Nothing shows *which words or features* drive a removal. That is exactly the gap our feature-attribution audit (SHAP, LIME, Integrated Gradients) is meant to fill.
3. **A ready-made test case for attribution.** The "reporting page" false positives and the Topical-vs-Toxic boundary are ideal cases for explanation. If attributions show the model keying on surface words (e.g. slurs quoted in a news report) rather than intent, that's concrete evidence for our audit.
4. **The political angle is in here.** "Ideological Harm – Toxic" explicitly includes "political radicalization content", and its example is a Reddit post about conservatives. That category also has the weakest F1. So this filter's political judgements are both the least reliable and unexplained, which ties directly to our working title.

## Concerns / why we should be careful with it

- **Labels are model-generated:** HarmFormer's training data and the 3M-page prevalence figures come from TTP (GPT-4o), not humans. Any bias in the prompt's judgement of "toxic" is passed on and then reported as fact. The only human-labelled set is small (491 pages, 2 annotators from the same company).
- **The taxonomy is itself a value judgement:** what counts as "ideological harm" or "political radicalization" is decided by the authors, and the examples are one-sided. As with Gururangan et al., this filter encodes its own ideology. We shouldn't treat its labels as neutral ground truth.
- **Reproducibility:** TTP depends on a closed model (GPT-4o) and is tuned to it. Other LLMs perform badly with the same prompt, and the GitHub link wasn't in our copy.
- **Narrow scope:** English only, and the authors note it doesn't handle culture-specific harms. It doesn't measure how filtering affects *which groups or dialects* lose data, so it isn't evidence of demographic bias on its own.
- **Some headline claims are stronger than the evidence:** e.g. "proves their superiority" on the OpenAI Moderation set, where HarmFormer's F1 (0.73) is only modestly above Llama Guard's (0.66–0.70).

## Where this ties back into XAI module content

- **Black-box vs. interpretable models (week ___):** HarmFormer is a deep black-box classifier making high-stakes data decisions with no explanation. It contrasts with the linear quality filter in Gururangan et al., where the coefficients are the explanation.
- **Post-hoc local explanations: SHAP, LIME, Integrated Gradients (week ___):** each removal decision is a local prediction we could explain at token level. Integrated Gradients fits because HarmFormer is a differentiable transformer. LIME/SHAP could also be applied to the TTP prompt as a black box.
- **Global explanations / model auditing (week ___):** aggregating token attributions across the Ideological Harm and Hate & Violence heads could show what the filter has learned overall, e.g. whether political or identity terms are driving "toxic" labels.
- **Faithfulness and evaluating explanations (week ___):** the reporting-page false positives give a natural sanity check. A faithful explanation should point to the quoted harmful words, not to the reporting framing.
