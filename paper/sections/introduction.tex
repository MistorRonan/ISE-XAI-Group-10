\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
 
Data filters decide whose language large language models learn from. C4's blocklist, for example, removed 42\% of documents classified as African American English but only 6.2\% of those classified as White-aligned English, and documents mentioning sexual orientation were among the most likely to be dropped \cite{dodge2021documenting}. Quality and toxicity classifiers show related patterns \cite{gururangan2022whose,sap2019risk}. Yet such filters are audited only in aggregate: we learn which groups lose text, but not why any single document was removed. A blocklist's reason is the listed word; a learned filter such as Detoxify \cite{detoxify} or HarmFormer \cite{mendu2025safer} offers none.
 
An auditor would therefore turn to post-hoc feature attribution, chiefly SHAP \cite{lundberg2017unified} and LIME \cite{ribeiro2016why}. But a post-hoc explanation is a simpler model of the model and can be wrong \cite{rudin2019stop}, and explainers often disagree with one another \cite{krishna2022disagreement}. Two explainers agreeing does not make either correct. Before an audit relies on them, they must be shown to be \emph{faithful}, meaning that they reflect what the filter actually did \cite{jacovi2020faithfully}.
 
We ask: \textbf{are SHAP and LIME faithful enough to support identity-term bias audits of a pretraining data filter, and when do they stop being trustworthy?} We test them on Detoxify against references whose correct answer we can compute independently. Our contributions are: (i)~a counterfactual reference built from minimal-pair identity-word swaps; (ii)~an exact-Shapley reference from a transparent filter; (iii)~a deletion-based comparison of SHAP, LIME and random words; and (iv)~a test of whether signal strength, LIME's fit quality or SHAP--LIME disagreement can warn an auditor that an explanation is unreliable. We find that attribution is faithful when the identity signal is strong and degrades when it is weak, so it can flag identity-term bias but cannot certify a filter as fair.
