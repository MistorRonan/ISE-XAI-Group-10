## TL;DR

The first detailed documentation of C4, the filtered Common Crawl corpus used to train T5. The authors look at where the text comes from, what is in it, and what the filters removed. The key result for us is that the blocklist ("bad words") filter disproportionately removes text from and about minority individuals. The paper also finds machine-generated text and benchmark evaluation examples inside C4, and a lot of text from unexpected sources such as patents and US military websites.

## Key takeaways

- **Method:** three levels of documentation: metadata (source domains), content (what the text is), and the text the filters excluded.
- **Main finding for us:** blocklist filtering disproportionately removes text from and about minority individuals. Our slide describes this as LGBTQ+ and African American English text; ___ (confirm exact groups and figures against the paper).
- **Other findings:** machine-generated text and benchmark test examples in the corpus (contamination).
- **Main argument:** web-scale corpora are released with almost no documentation, and filter choices are not neutral, so they need to be documented and audited.

## Why it matters for our project

1. **Blocklist case study.** The concrete example of a simple keyword filter causing harm, used on slide 2.
2. **Explainability gap.** The filter is a transparent word list, but nobody can see which individual documents were lost or why. The audit is in aggregate.
3. **Testable.** A blocklist is a simple filter we could probe with our identity-swap experiment (EXP2).

## Concerns / why we should be careful with it

- **Scope:** one corpus (English C4, April 2021 Common Crawl snapshot) and one blocklist.
- **Age:** 2021; newer corpora (e.g. FineWeb) use different filters.
