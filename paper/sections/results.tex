\noindent\textbf{Faithfulness to the References.}
% Sources: exp3_faithfulness_shap.csv, exp3_faithfulness_lime.csv,
% exp3_results.csv (share of points above y = x: 83% original, 75% unbiased).
% Exp 2. Sources: exp2_results.csv (p, phi_share), exp1_results.csv + exp2_agreement.csv (top1_agree).
% Cut for space: unbiased toxicity 0.106 vs 0.002; n = 150 and 40; ~20% share on a control word.
In the identity-term swap (Exp~2), identity sentences scored far higher than control sentences (mean toxicity 0.124 vs.\ 0.001 for \texttt{original}), SHAP placed 46\% (\texttt{original}) and 60\% (\texttt{unbiased}) of its absolute attribution on the identity word, and SHAP and LIME chose the same top word in 78 of 88 sentences.
Table~\ref{tab:faith} summarises every faithfulness result. In the counterfactual test (Exp~3), the true effect of identity term $t$ and SHAP's estimate of it are
\begin{equation}
\Delta_t = \ell(x_t) - \overline{\ell(x_c)}, \qquad
\hat{\Delta}_t = \phi_t - \overline{\phi_c},
\label{eq:cf}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the filter's log-odds, $\phi$ is a SHAP value and the bars denote the mean over the four control-word versions $x_c$ of the same template. Fig.~\ref{fig:cf} plots $\hat{\Delta}_t$ against $\Delta_t$. SHAP ranks the effects almost perfectly ($\rho = 0.96$ for \texttt{original}, 0.92 for \texttt{unbiased}; 0.98 and 0.95 per term) and gives the correct sign in 98\% and 93\% of sentences. Its magnitudes are less accurate: the mean absolute error is 0.94 and 0.72 log-odds, and 83\% and 75\% of points lie above $y = x$, so SHAP tends to overstate the effect.

Deleting removed words or using the mask token instead of ``...'' barely changes the ranking ($\rho \geq 0.97$ between baselines) but lowers SHAP's error from 0.94 log-odds (``...'') to 0.84 (deletion) and 0.70 (mask token) for \texttt{original} (0.72, 0.58 and 0.44 for \texttt{unbiased}).

On the transparent filter (Exp~4), SHAP's Partition approximation matched the exact Shapley values almost perfectly (median $\rho = 0.993$, MAE 0.022 log-odds), and LIME's top word matched the exact one in 96\% of runs. The filter's own coefficients are positive for 14 of the 15 identity words (e.g.\ gay $+2.83$, muslim $+1.90$ log-odds) and negative for all four control words ($-0.26$ to $-0.39$).

\noindent\textbf{Deletion Test.}
% Exp 5 (Bláithín). Sources: exp5_aopc.csv, exp5_aopc_by_group.csv.
% Cut for space: SHAP gap largest on Exp 1 counter / report sentences (4.58 /

Deleting the top-ranked words lowered the toxic log-odds far more than deleting random words (Table~\ref{tab:faith}; 4.02, 4.06 and 0.95 for \texttt{unbiased}). The gap was largest on the Exp~1 counter and report sentences and close to zero on control sentences. Sufficiency was negative for both explainers but positive for random words, so the top-ranked words alone scored higher than the full sentence, most strongly in report sentences ($-3.4$).

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Where the Explainers Break.}
% Sources: exp6_summary.csv (changed), exp6_stability_by_sentence.csv
% (correlations; halves = median split on signal, 119 sentences each,
% n = 5000; recomputed 2026-10-22), exp6_warning_signs.csv.
In the stability test (Exp~6), LIME's top word changes between seeds in 12\% of sentences at 500 samples and in 4\% at 5000. Each sentence's instability (1 minus the mean Spearman $\rho$ between runs) is unrelated to its signal strength $s = |\ell(x) - \phi_0|$, where $\phi_0$ is SHAP's base value ($|\rho| < 0.05$). However, signal strength does predict how well LIME's surrogate fits ($\rho = 0.73$ with $R^2$): at 5000 samples the mean $R^2$ is 0.41 for the weaker half of sentences against 0.77 for the stronger half, and LIME's top word matches SHAP's in 86\% against 95\%.

Among cues that need no reference, weak signal strength best identified the 25\% of identity sentences with the largest SHAP error $|\Delta_t - \hat{\Delta}_t|$ (AUC 0.80; 0.5 is chance), followed by low LIME $R^2$ (0.64), while SHAP--LIME disagreement did no better than chance (0.46).
