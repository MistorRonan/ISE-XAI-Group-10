\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
 
Data filters decide whose text large language models learn from. C4's blocklist, for example, removed 42\% of documents classified as African American English but only 6.2\% of those classified as White-aligned English \cite{dodge2021documenting}. The same study found that documents mentioning sexual orientation were among the most likely to be dropped. Quality and toxicity classifiers show related patterns \cite{gururangan2022whose,sap2019risk}. However, such filters are audited only in aggregate: we learn which groups lose text, but not why any single document was removed. A blocklist's reason for removal is the listed word it matched, whereas a learned filter such as Detoxify \cite{detoxify} or HarmFormer \cite{mendu2025safer} gives no reason.
 
An auditor would therefore turn to post-hoc feature attribution, chiefly SHAP \cite{lundberg2017unified} and LIME \cite{ribeiro2016why}. However, a post-hoc explanation only approximates the model and can be wrong \cite{rudin2019stop}, and explainers often disagree with one another \cite{krishna2022disagreement}. Agreement between two explainers does not make either correct. Before an audit relies on them, they must be shown to be \emph{faithful}, meaning they reflect what the filter did \cite{jacovi2020faithfully}.
 
We ask: \textbf{are SHAP and LIME faithful enough to support identity-term bias audits of a pretraining data filter, and when do they stop being trustworthy?} We test them on Detoxify against references whose correct answers we can compute independently. Our contributions are: (i)~a counterfactual reference built from minimal-pair identity-word swaps; (ii)~an exact-Shapley reference from a transparent filter; (iii)~a deletion-based comparison of SHAP, LIME and random words; and (iv)~a test of whether simple cues can warn an auditor that an explanation is unreliable. The cues are signal strength, LIME's fit quality and SHAP--LIME disagreement. We find that attribution is faithful when the identity signal is strong and degrades when it is weak, so it can flag identity-term bias but cannot certify a filter as fair.
