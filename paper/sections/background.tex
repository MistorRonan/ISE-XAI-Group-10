\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:background}

\noindent\textbf{Bias in Pretraining Data Filters.}
Beyond blocklist and classifier biases (Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}), filters also misread context.
Mendu et al.\ found that a transformer-based filter flags news articles that \emph{report} on harmful content~\cite{mendu2025safer}.
Udagawa et al.\ found that a fairness-oriented regard classifier removes sentences reporting real-world events involving marginalised groups~\cite{udagawa2025bias}.
Dixon et al.\ isolated unintended classifier bias with minimal-pair templates that swap one identity word for a neutral control (e.g.\ ``my neighbour is \emph{gay}'' vs.\ ``\emph{tall}'')~\cite{dixon2018measuring}, a design we reuse.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Feature Attribution for Text.}
SHAP attributes a prediction using Shapley values, the unique attribution satisfying efficiency, symmetry and the null-player axiom~\cite{lundberg2017unified}.
For text, the SHAP library uses a partition approximation; exact values can be computed only for short sentences and serve as our reference (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}).
LIME instead fits a locally weighted sparse linear surrogate to perturbations of the input, with the surrogate's $R^2$ measuring local fidelity~\cite{ribeiro2016why}.
Both are post-hoc, local and model-agnostic, needing only query access~\cite{munn2022xai,molnar2022iml}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Evaluating Explanations: Faithfulness vs.\ Agreement.}
An explanation is \emph{faithful} if it reflects the model's actual reasoning, which is distinct from looking plausible to a human~\cite{jacovi2020faithfully}; our tests are \emph{functionally grounded}, using formal proxies rather than human studies~\cite{doshivelez2017rigorous}.
ERASER's deletion metrics, comprehensiveness and sufficiency~\cite{deyoung2020eraser}, are one such proxy, but deleting words creates out-of-distribution inputs, so score changes may partly reflect distribution shift~\cite{hase2021ood}.
Explainers also disagree with one another~\cite{krishna2022disagreement}, can be manipulated to hide discriminatory behaviour~\cite{slack2020fooling}, and are not necessarily most faithful on the most accurate classifier~\cite{ngueajio2025decoding}.
These evaluations used general classification tasks; to our knowledge, no prior work has evaluated attribution faithfulness in pretraining filter audits against references whose correct answers are known independently.
