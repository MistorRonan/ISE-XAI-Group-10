Pretraining data filters silently decide whose language large language models learn from, and have been shown to remove text from specific groups. They are audited only in aggregate, and the feature-attribution methods an auditor would use to explain individual removals have not been tested for faithfulness. We evaluate SHAP and LIME on the Detoxify filter with four tests: (i)~agreement with counterfactual identity-word swaps, (ii)~agreement with exact Shapley values on a transparent filter, (iii)~a deletion test, and (iv)~a stability test. Both explainers rank identity-word effects almost as the counterfactual does (Spearman $\rho \geq 0.92$ for SHAP, 0.97 for LIME) and recover exact Shapley values ($\rho \geq 0.98$), but SHAP overstates the size of the effect, and its error depends on how removed words are replaced. On weak signals LIME's surrogate fit falls ($R^2$ 0.77 to 0.41), and SHAP--LIME disagreement is no better than chance (AUC 0.46) at flagging wrong explanations. Attribution can therefore triage a filter for identity-term bias, but cannot certify it as fair.
